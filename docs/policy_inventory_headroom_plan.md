## Question

Before building a stochastic Gaussian-game pilot, determine whether the exact
continuous policy-inventory action has at least 5% material certified utility
over strong global causal shrinkage rules.  This is a one-event drift ceiling
over deterministic workload rays.  It is not return, MSE, sample-efficiency or
standard-MARL evidence.

## Frozen design

The 72 rays cross:

- distinct actor count `n in {3,5,8}`;
- workload `steady_low`, `steady_high`, `alternating`, and `bursty`;
- inventory direction `away`, `mixed`, and `rotating`;
- policy variance `0.75` and `1.25`;
- 72 declared completion events per ray.

Signal decays deterministically with a public oscillation.  Completed and
pending log-second-moment risks, directional linear terms and quadratic policy
geometry are generated by the public formulas in the runner.  All declared
states obey nonnegative exact Gaussian geometry on the full trust interval.

The controller is compared with 29 global baseline rules: the certified base
step, three fixed base-step scales, five pending-count shrinkages, five total
risk shrinkages, five absolute-drift shrinkages, five count thresholds and five
risk thresholds.  Every comparator receives the same exact pointwise no-harm
fallback to zero.  The best baseline is selected per workload ray, not per
event.  The dynamic controller minimizes the exact scalar drift per event.

## Frozen gates

All six gates are mandatory:

1. all declared state-policy rows are complete and finite;
2. aggregate dynamic / best-ray-baseline certified utility is at least 1.05;
3. at least 60% of rays obtain at least 5% utility gain;
4. at least 99% of zero-pending states recover the base certified step;
5. at least 30% of maximum-inventory states take a nonzero update;
6. the continuous dynamic action pointwise dominates every selected ray-level
   baseline up to numerical tolerance.

Failure of any gate stops this policy-inventory paper candidate before a
stochastic pilot.  Passing all gates authorizes only completion of the Markov
ratio/confidence theorem and a separately committed stochastic CPU pilot.
