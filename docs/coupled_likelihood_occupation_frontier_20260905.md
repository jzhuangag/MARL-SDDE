# Coupled likelihood-occupation frontier under expected safety

Date: 2026-09-05

Status: **exact variational characterization for the finite-commit Gaussian
common-factor experiment; not a finite-variable LP and not an asynchronous
interleaved-MARL theorem.**

## Why two independent occupation measures are wrong

Under the two regimes, the action distributions must be generated by the same
predictable randomized policy.  Optimizing two unrelated occupation measures
would let the regime-zero process use its own oracle and the regime-one process
use a different oracle from the first round.  That relaxation erases the
adaptation cost that the lower bound is supposed to measure.

Writing equality of the two conditional action kernels directly produces
ratio or cross-product constraints between two measures.  The likelihood
ratio supplies a cleaner exact coupling.

## Likelihood-augmented controlled state

Use regime zero as reference and define

\[
L_t=
\frac{d\mathbb P_1^\pi|_{\mathcal F_t}}
     {d\mathbb P_0^\pi|_{\mathcal F_t}}.
\]

For the Gaussian common-factor Markov experiment, a sufficient state is

\[
X_t=(m_{0,t},P_{0,t},m_{1,t},P_{1,t},L_t,M_t,E_t,s_t).
\tag{1}
\]

The pairs \((m_{j,t},P_{j,t})\) are the two public Kalman filter states;
\(M_t,E_t\) are the remaining message and environment budgets; and \(s_t\)
contains the delay bookkeeping needed to determine admissible actions and
terminal usability.  An action \(A_{t+1}\) is drawn from one measurable kernel
\(\kappa(da\mid X_t)\).  Under the reference law,

\[
Y_{t+1}\sim p_0(\cdot\mid X_t,A_{t+1}),
\qquad
L_{t+1}=L_t
\frac{p_1(Y_{t+1}\mid X_t,A_{t+1})}
     {p_0(Y_{t+1}\mid X_t,A_{t+1})}.
\tag{2}
\]

Support and finite-budget assumptions guarantee that (2) is well defined and
that stopping occurs by a deterministic finite upper bound.

The physical action catalogue can be finite, but the posterior means and
likelihood ratio are continuous.  Consequently, (1) is finite-dimensional
while its exact occupation variable is a measure on a continuous state space.
Any finite grid or ordinary finite-variable LP is an approximation.

## Lemma 1: exact coupling by one reference measure

Let \(\mu_0^\pi\) and \(\mu_1^\pi\) be the pre-stopping state-action occupation
measures under the two regimes.  For every nonnegative measurable test
function (f), bounded stopping and the Radon--Nikodym identity give

\[
\int f(x,a)\,\mu_1^\pi(dx,da)
=
\int L(x)f(x,a)\,\mu_0^\pi(dx,da).
\tag{3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\mu_1^\pi(dx,da)=L(x)\mu_0^\pi(dx,da).
\]

Because the action kernel cancels from the likelihood ratio before the next
observation is drawn, (3) preserves the requirement that the same predictable
kernel acts under both regimes.

## Theorem 1: exact expected-safety occupation program

Let \(K_0\) be the transient transition kernel induced by (2) under regime
zero.  Let \(\mu\) be a nonnegative pre-stopping state-action occupation
measure and \(\eta\) a nonnegative terminal state-deployment measure.  They
satisfy

\[
\mu_X+\eta_X=\delta_{x_0}+\mu K_0,
\tag{4}
\]

together with the state-dependent support constraints encoding the two
budgets, delay, and proper stopping.

Let (c_j(x,a)) be a predictable running cost paid at the decision state and
\(\ell_j(x,u)\) the terminal deployment loss.  Post-observation costs can be
represented as next-state costs, in which case they carry (L_{t+1}) rather
than (L_t).  Define

\[
S_0(\mu,\eta)
=\int c_0\,d\mu+\int\ell_0\,d\eta-R_0^{\rm all},
\tag{5}
\]

\[
G_1(\mu,\eta)
=\int Lc_1\,d\mu+\int L\ell_1\,d\eta-R_1^\star.
\tag{6}
\]

Then the minimum regime-one regret under regime-zero expected safety is

\[
\mathcal V_B(\epsilon)
=\inf_{\mu,\eta\ge0}
\left\{
G_1(\mu,\eta):
(4),\ \operatorname{supp}(\mu,\eta)\subseteq\mathcal A_B,
\ S_0(\mu,\eta)\le\epsilon
\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]

Under standard-Borel state/action spaces, finite-budget transience, proper
stopping, and integrable costs, (7) is an exact variational characterization
over admissible predictable policies, not merely a relaxation.

### Proof skeleton

1. AC-7 supplies the likelihood martingale and bounded stopped
   change-of-measure identity.
2. Equation (2) makes the pair of hypothesis filters, likelihood ratio,
   budgets, and delay bookkeeping a controlled Markov state under regime zero.
3. Every admissible policy produces measures satisfying (4); (3) converts all
   regime-one expectations to the linear expressions in (6).
4. Conversely, disintegrate a feasible measure as
   \(\kappa(da\mid x)=\mu(dx,da)/\mu_X(dx)\), with an arbitrary admissible
   kernel on null states.  The flow equation reconstructs the regime-zero law.
5. Since (L_t) is a component of (X_t), conditioning by it yields the same
   action kernel under regime one and reconstructs (3).  Thus the reconstructed
   policy attains (5)--(6).

The sufficient-state Markovization remains valid for history-dependent
policies: conditional on (X_t), multiplication by the state-measurable
likelihood ratio does not change the conditional distribution of the next
action.

## Corollary 1: a finite-budget safety-slack lower bound

Assume the separated finite-deployment and noncancellation conditions in
`learning_excess_to_testing_reduction_20260905.md`, with wrong-deployment gaps
\(g_0,g_1>0\).  Let

\[
K_B^{\max}
=\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_B}
\operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_1^\pi|_{\mathcal F_\tau}
\Vert\mathbb P_0^\pi|_{\mathcal F_\tau})<\infty.
\tag{8}
\]

For policies whose regime-zero safety excess is at most \(\epsilon\) and whose
regime-one regret is at most (r), the deployment decoder has errors
\(\alpha\le\epsilon/g_0\) and \(\beta\le r/g_1\).  Bretagnolle--Huber and
data processing imply

\[
\alpha+\beta\ge\frac12e^{-K_B^{\max}}.
\]

Therefore the minimum required expected safety slack obeys

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_B^\star(r)
\ge
g_0\left[
\frac12e^{-K_B^{\max}}-\frac r{g_1}
\right]_+ .}
\tag{9}
\]

A sharper implicit form uses

\[
\psi_I(\beta)
=\inf\{\alpha\in[0,1-\beta):
\operatorname{kl}(1-\beta,\alpha)\le I\}
\]

to give

\[
\epsilon_B^\star(r)
\ge g_0\psi_{K_B^{\max}}(r/g_1).
\tag{10}
\]

In particular, exact expected no-harm at finite budget entails

\[
r\ge\frac{g_1}{2}e^{-K_B^{\max}}>0.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus finite-budget safety and adaptation have an unavoidable opportunity
tradeoff even without imposing a pathwise-safe sensing catalogue.  The result
correctly becomes vacuous if the regimes have a common near-optimal deployment
and a separation gap tends to zero.

## What remains open

- Computing (7) exactly requires a continuous-state occupation solver or
  dynamic program.  A finite discretization would need approximation-error
  bounds.
- Equations (9)--(11) are lower bounds, not a matching achievable policy.
- The baseline-as-sensing anytime likelihood switch has not yet been proved to
  attain the frontier or its budget order.
- The explicit deployment-gap corollary fails without fresh deployment or an
  alternative noncancellation theorem.
- A continuously interleaved asynchronous-MARL learner need not expose a
  finite terminal deployment decision and is not covered.

The remaining publication-level gate is a positive, executable upper policy
that matches the coupled frontier to a stated order on a nondegenerate class.
