# Why a dynamic signed cache action can be strictly necessary

## Separating factored Markov instance

This construction is a proof of nonzero algorithmic headroom, not a benchmark
result. An owner has scalar parameter `x>=0` and two equally old, equally
displaced eligible teammate caches. A binary Markov relevance state `Z_t` has
stationary initial distribution and persistence `p`. Refreshing the matching
edge produces a clipped owner packet `g=+delta`; refreshing the other produces
`g=-delta`; no refresh produces zero. Both positive actions cost one unit, so
age, mismatch and communication price cannot distinguish them. After each
event both donor policies advance versions, regenerating the two equal cache
displacements; this models faster teammate clocks rather than resetting the
owner learning state.

An explicit smooth local-factor embedding uses cache-freshness coordinates
`u_1,u_2` in `{0,1}` and Markov sign `s` in `{-1,+1}`:

\[
 \ell_s(x,u_1,u_2)
 =s\delta x(u_1-u_2)+\frac12x^2u_1u_2.
\]

With neither edge refreshed, its owner gradient is zero. Either one-edge
refresh gives `+delta` or `-delta`, with the sign swapped by `s`. Refreshing
both recovers gradient `x` of the current cooperative potential `F(x)=x^2/2`
but costs two units and is infeasible under the unit per-event budget. Thus the
table is realized by a polynomial three-policy local factor, not an arbitrary
hand-written score oracle.

The cooperative potential is `F(x)=x^2/2`. For a selected packet, its exact
one-step change is

\[
 F(x-\alpha g)-F(x)=-\alpha xg+\frac12\alpha^2g^2.
\tag{1}
\]

Therefore the joint Lyapunov minimizer is

\[
 \alpha^*(g)=\Pi_{[0,\bar\alpha]}(xg/g^2).
\tag{2}
\]

It assigns positive weight to the matching packet and zero weight to the
anti-aligned packet. Its signed edge action chooses `a_t=Z_t`. This instance
can be embedded as a local quadratic factor of a three-policy cooperative
Markov game; the Markov state changes the sign of the two cache-induced owner
gradient components while leaving their ages and norms identical.

## Strict equal-resource gap

Use `x_0=1`, `delta=0.25`, `bar(alpha)=0.5`, horizon four and persistence
`p=0.9`. Exact enumeration covers all 16 Markov paths. The proposed controller
and every positive baseline spend exactly four refresh units. Comparators are
both fixed edges, the stronger fixed edge selected after observing the initial
state, and both phases of round robin. No refresh is reported separately at
zero cost.

The state-aware signed controller makes a correct update in all four events.
Any fixed or age-only rule has a positive probability of presenting an
anti-aligned packet; even though the optimal scalar shield rejects that packet,
the event and communication opportunity are lost. Consequently the proposed
terminal potential is `0.125`. The strongest comparator fixes the edge after
observing the initial Markov state and attains `0.1670625`; the proposed ratio
is `0.748223`, a **25.1777%** reduction at exactly four refresh units each.
Both unconditional fixed edges attain `0.3054375`, and both round-robin phases
attain `0.2826875`. The exact numeric gap is generated by exhaustive enumeration in
`factor_switch_separation.py` and regression-tested.

## What this proves and what it does not

The construction proves that dynamic signed cache scheduling has an intrinsic
role that cannot be reduced to fixed topology, age, mismatch magnitude or
adaptive step size alone. The step-size shield prevents harmful application,
but it cannot recover a communication opportunity spent on the wrong edge;
the graph action and packet weight are complementary decisions.

It does not prove that a neural critic can identify the sign, that Pursuit has
the same effect size, or that the method beats learned online schedulers. Those
claims require a simultaneous Markov/factor confidence bound and independent
standard-benchmark evidence. The construction is retained even if the deep
extension later fails because it is an exact boundary result, not a tuned toy
experiment.
